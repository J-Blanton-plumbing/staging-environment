Pipe Burst Right Now?

What to Do When a Pipe Bursts

Do these three things first. They limit the damage while we're on the way.

1. Shut Off the Main

Your main shutoff valve is usually near the water meter in the basement or a utility room. Turn it clockwise to stop water to the whole house.

2. Cut Power to Wet Areas

If water is near outlets, appliances or your electrical panel, stay out of the water and turn off power to that area only if you can do it safely.

3. Call Us 24/7

Our phone line is open nights, weekends and holidays. Tell us where the water is coming from and we'll take it from there.

Know the Signs

Signs of a Burst Pipe

A burst pipe isn't always water spraying across the basement. When a pipe splits inside a wall, a ceiling or under a floor, the signs are quieter: stains, bubbling paint, warped floors and a water bill that suddenly jumps. Here's where to look:

On Walls and Ceilings

  • Yellow, brown or dark water stains on plaster, drywall or ceiling panels.
  • Paint that bubbles, cracks or flakes, or wallpaper that starts to peel.
  • Drywall or plaster that sags, bows or feels soft to the touch.
  • Mold or mildew along baseboards and in wall corners.

On Floors and Baseboards

  • Hardwood that cups or buckles, or laminate and vinyl that lifts.
  • Wood baseboards that swell, crack or lose their paint.
  • Puddles on the floor, drips from the ceiling or water coming out from under cabinets and vanities.

Around Exposed Pipes

  • Water spraying, dripping or gushing from a crack, joint or pinhole.
  • A hissing sound near a pipe.
  • Wet spots or puddles under pipes in the basement or crawl space.

Other Clues

  • A water bill that jumps with no change in how you use water.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • A water meter that keeps moving when nothing is running.
  • Water pressure that suddenly drops.

Seeing water come from a wall or ceiling? Shut off the main and call us 24/7, even if you're not sure it's a burst pipe.

Before It Bursts

Signs of a Frozen Pipe

A frozen pipe almost always gives you a warning before it bursts. As the water inside freezes, pressure builds until the pipe splits, and the leak usually shows up once it thaws. Watch for these signs during a cold snap:

No Water or Just a Trickle

A faucet that gives you nothing, or a thin trickle, on a very cold day usually means the pipe feeding it has frozen somewhere along the way.

Frost or Ice on the Pipe

Frost, ice or heavy condensation on pipes in a basement, crawl space or garage is a pipe on the edge of freezing solid.

A Bulge or Small Split

A visible bulge or hairline split in an exposed pipe means the ice has already stretched it. It may hold until it thaws, then leak.

Think a pipe is frozen? Keep the faucet open, never thaw it with an open flame, and call us before it thaws and splits.

Local Knowledge

Why Pipes Freeze in Highland Park Homes

Lake County winters bring long stretches below freezing, and about 1 in 4 Highland Park homes was built before 1940. That combination is hard on pipes.

Pipes in Exterior Walls and Crawl Spaces

Pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces, garages and outdoor spigot lines freeze first. Older homes often have plumbing in spots that weren't insulated the way they would be today.

Outdoor Spigots

A hose left connected in winter traps water in the spigot line, and that water freezes and splits the pipe inside the wall. It often isn't found until the spring thaw.

Older Pipe Materials

Galvanized steel and older copper lines are already thinner or corroded after decades of use, so a freeze is more likely to split them. If breaks keep happening, it may be time to talk about the pipes themselves.
