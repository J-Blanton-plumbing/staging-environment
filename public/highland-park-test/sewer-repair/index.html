Know the Signs

Does Your Sewer Line Need Repair?

A sewer line problem doesn't always mean a full replacement. Most issues start smaller than that, and catching them early is what keeps them that way. Here's how to know where you stand:

Slow Drains or Gurgling That Keep Coming Back After Rodding

If rodding clears it but the same slow drain or gurgling returns within a few months, the clog isn't the real problem. Something further down the line is catching debris again.

Recurring Backups or Pooling Water in the Yard

A backup that keeps happening, or a soggy patch in the yard, points to something wrong with the pipe itself, not a one-time clog. Rodding alone probably won't hold this time.

Confirmed Crack, Joint Separation, or Root Intrusion on Camera

Once a camera inspection confirms damage to the pipe, this isn't a rodding job anymore. We'll walk you through what repair looks like for your line.

Local Knowledge

Why Sewer Lines Fail in Highland Park

The median home in Highland Park was built in 1963, and about 1 in 4 went up before 1940. Add wooded ravines and mature trees, and you get the three causes we'd check first on any Highland Park sewer line.

Clay and Cast Iron That Have Reached Their Age

Homes built before the 1970s often have clay or cast iron sewer lines. Clay typically lasts 50 to 60 years and is especially vulnerable to cracking at the joints. Cast iron usually lasts 50 to 75 years, but corrodes from the inside as it ages. Nearly half of Highland Park homes were built before 1960, so many lines here are at or past that range.

Roots From Ravines and Tree-Lined Streets

Roots grow toward any source of moisture, and a sewer line is exactly that. Once a root finds a crack or a loose joint, it keeps growing until it blocks the flow or cracks the pipe further. If roots are the only problem, hydro jetting can clear them. If they've already broken the pipe, it's a repair.

Freeze-Thaw and Shifting Ground

Lake County winters mean years of ground freezing and thawing around your pipe. Joints take the strain and can separate, or the pipe can settle into a low spot, called a belly, where waste collects instead of flowing through.

The Line Is Yours to Fix

In Highland Park, the homeowner owns and maintains the private sewer line from the house to the city main. When that line cracks or collapses, the repair is on you, not the city. That's why we show you the footage and the exact location of the damage before recommending anything.

Repair Methods

How We Repair a Sewer Line

Once we know what's wrong, there's usually more than one way to fix it. Which method makes sense depends on the pipe's condition, location, and how it failed.

MethodWhat It InvolvesTypical Disruption
Trenchless Pipe LiningA resin-coated liner is pulled through the existing pipe and cured in place, creating a new pipe inside the old one.Minimal digging, usually just small access points
Trenchless Pipe BurstingA new pipe is pulled through the old one while a bursting head fractures the old pipe outward to make room.Minimal digging, works even when the old pipe is badly damaged
Traditional Excavation (Spot Repair)The damaged section is dug up and replaced directly.More digging, but sometimes the only option depending on damage location and access

On wooded Highland Park lots, a method that saves mature trees and landscaping is often worth asking about. Your technician will walk you through which method fits your situation after the camera inspection.
