Know the Signs

When Your Faucet Needs Repair or Replacement

Most faucet problems start small. Here's when to call:

It Drips When It's Off

A worn cartridge, washer or seal is the usual cause. A steady drip wastes more water than most people think.

Low Pressure at One Faucet

If one faucet is weak and the rest are fine, the aerator or cartridge may be clogged. If every faucet is weak, it may be the pipes.

Leaks Under the Sink

Water in the cabinet usually comes from a supply line, a shutoff valve or the faucet base. Catch it before it damages the cabinet.

Hard to Turn or Loose Handles

Stiff, wobbly or grinding handles mean worn internal parts. Sometimes a repair, sometimes it's time for a new faucet.

Kitchen Faucets

Kitchen Faucet Repair and Installation

The kitchen faucet gets more use than any other fixture in the house. We repair drips, leaks, weak spray and loose handles, and we install new kitchen faucets, including pull-down and pull-out models, faucets with side sprayers and filtered water taps.

A new faucet is also a good time to replace the shutoff valves and supply lines under the sink. In older homes, those valves may not have been turned in years, and they're often the part that fails. We'll check them and tell you if they need replacing.

Local Knowledge

Faucets and Fixtures in Older Highland Park Homes

Nearly half of Highland Park homes were built before 1960. Older plumbing behind a new faucet can be the difference between a quick swap and a surprise.

Old Shutoff Valves

Shutoff valves that haven't been turned in decades can seize or start leaking the moment they're used. We check them first and replace them if they won't hold.

Galvanized Supply Lines

Older galvanized water lines can narrow with corrosion, which shows up as low pressure at new faucets. We'll tell you if the fixture or the pipe is the problem.
