Know the Basics

What Is Trenchless Sewer Repair?

Trenchless sewer repair, also called no-dig sewer repair, fixes a damaged pipe without digging a trench along its full length. Instead of opening up your yard from the house to the street, we work through one or two access points and repair the pipe from the inside or replace it in place.

There are two main methods: cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining and pipe bursting. Both fix the line underground, so what’s above it stays untouched.

Common Scenarios

When Does Trenchless Apply?

Backups, slow drains, sewer odor, soft or unusually lush patches of grass, and gurgling drains are the most common signs of a damaged sewer line. What they don’t tell you is which repair method fits. That comes down to the type and extent of the damage.

Situation Trenchless Usually Works Needs Traditional Excavation
Root intrusion cracking the pipe Yes Not typically
Corroded or aging clay, cast iron, or Orangeburg pipe Yes Not typically
Minor cracks or offset joints Yes Not typically
Slow drains or backups traced to pipe damage Usually, after a camera inspection confirms it Not typically
Fully collapsed section of pipe Not typically Yes
Severe belly (a low spot where the pipe has sunk) Not typically Yes
Major structural misalignment Not typically Yes

Not sure which category applies to your line? A camera inspection shows exactly what’s happening underground before any repair method gets recommended.

The Two Methods

How Trenchless Sewer Repair Works

Cured-in-Place Pipe (CIPP) Lining

We insert a resin-coated liner into the damaged pipe through the access point, then cure it in place so it hardens into a new pipe inside the old one. Every truck runs light-cured lining technology, which cures faster and produces a stronger finished liner than older UV, water, or steam-based methods.

Pipe Bursting

For pipes that are too damaged for lining, we pull a new pipe through the old one while breaking the old pipe apart around it. The line gets replaced end to end without opening a trench.

Why Trenchless

Why Homeowners Choose Trenchless Over Traditional Excavation

Traditional sewer repair means digging a trench the length of the damaged pipe. Trenchless repair works through one or two small access points instead.

  • Minimal disruption. You’re left with a couple of small patches of disturbed ground, not a trench across your whole yard.
  • Faster turnaround. We keep a full lining and bursting fleet on our own trailers, so we’re never waiting on a rental company or another crew. Every truck uses light-cured lining technology, which cures faster than older UV, water, or steam-based methods.
  • Lower overall cost. No trench means no landscaping, driveway, or sidewalk restoration afterward. We also check our pricing against national industry data, so you know the number we give you is fair.

Check Your Fit

Is Trenchless Right for Your Home?

Most residential lines qualify for trenchless repair. But older lines, especially clay, cast iron, or Orangeburg pipe, often have more than one weak point, so fixing the obvious problem doesn’t always mean the whole line is covered.

Sometimes it’s part trenchless, part traditional dig. Only a camera inspection tells you which one applies to your home, before any repair method gets recommended.
