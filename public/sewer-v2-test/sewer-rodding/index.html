The Basics

What Is Sewer Rodding?

Sewer rodding clears a blocked main line with a long, flexible steel cable fed through a cleanout access point. A cutting head on the end breaks through roots, grease, and debris and pushes the blockage toward the street connection, restoring flow through the pipe. It’s the standard first fix for a clogged or backed-up sewer line. For most homes, it’s the only fix needed.

Know the Signs

When You Need Sewer Rodding

Sewer rodding fixes a blocked or clogged main line, not a minor clog. Here’s how to know it’s time to call:

You Have an Active Backup

Water or sewage coming up through a basement floor drain or laundry drain means your main line is blocked. Call now for same-day emergency rodding, evenings, weekends, and holidays.

Your Line Keeps Backing Up

If you’ve had your sewer rodded more than once in the past year, roots or a pipe defect are likely growing back between visits.

Tried a Snake and It Didn’t Work?

A handheld drain snake can clear a sink or tub drain, but it doesn’t have the reach or cutting power to break through a main sewer line clog.

Homes with a history of root intrusion may benefit from rodding every six months. Your technician starts by running a camera through the line to see exactly what's causing the backup, roots, grease, a pipe belly, whatever it is. You see the footage before any work begins. Once you know what you're dealing with, you get a flat price for the rodding, and it doesn't change once work starts. A professional-grade sewer rod will clear most residential clogs. A handheld hardware-store snake typically won't.

A professional-grade sewer rod (sometimes called a power snake) will clear most residential sewer line clogs. A handheld drain snake from a hardware store typically won't. It's designed for sink and tub drains, not the 4-inch or 6-inch main sewer pipe. If you've already tried a hardware-store snake without success, the problem is in the main line and needs professional rodding equipment.

Tree root intrusion, grease and sediment buildup, pipe bellies, and deterioration of older clay or cast-iron pipes.

The most common residential sewer line issues are tree root intrusion, grease and sediment buildup, pipe bellies (low spots where water pools), and deterioration of older clay or cast-iron pipes. In Chicago specifically, freeze-thaw cycles and soil shifting add stress to aging pipes. A camera inspection during a routine rodding visit identifies which of these you're dealing with before they cause a full backup.

Rodding clears the blockage and restores flow. Hydro jetting scours the whole pipe wall clean, which matters if buildup keeps coming back.

Rodding breaks through roots, debris, or a clog and restores flow fast, it's the right call for a first-time backup or a line that's never been serviced. Hydro jetting scours the entire pipe wall clean, not just the blockage, so if you've had the same line rodded more than once in the past year, jetting is worth discussing with your technician.
