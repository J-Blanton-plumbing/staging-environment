Common Scenarios

When You Need a New Sewer Line Installed

Sewer line installation means a brand-new line, whether that's a first connection or a full system upgrade, not a repair to one you already have. Here's what that looks like:

New Construction or an Addition

Building a new structure or adding to your property means connecting to the sewer system for the first time. We plan that connection as part of the build, not an afterthought.

Converting to an ADU or Finishing a Basement

Turning a garage or basement into livable space, or building an accessory dwelling unit, usually means adding fixtures that need their own sewer connection.

A Property That's Never Had a Sewer Connection

Some properties still run on a septic system or were never connected to the city sewer at all. Getting a proper connection in place is installation, not a repair.

A Plumber Told You It's Time for a Full System Upgrade

When a line has failed for good, patching it again isn't an option. A full system upgrade means a new line instead of patching the old one again.

Re-Routing a Line That's in the Way

A new addition, garage, driveway, or pool built over your sewer line's current path means the line has to move, even if it's still in good shape. We run a new line along a route that works with your property as it is now.

Our Sewer Line Installation Process

A new sewer line touches your yard, your permits, and the city's approval before it's done. Here's how we handle each part:

  1. We start by walking the site, checking what's already there, and mapping out where the new line needs to run.
  2. We pull the required permits and handle the paperwork with your city or municipality, so it isn't something you have to sort out yourself.
  3. We install the line using the method that fits your site and your yard.
  4. Once it's in, we test the line and coordinate the required city inspection before we close everything up.
  5. We restore the site when the work's done, so you're not left with an open trench or a torn-up yard.

From the first look at your site to the city's final sign-off, you know what's happening and why at every step.

  • Permit handling with your city or municipality
