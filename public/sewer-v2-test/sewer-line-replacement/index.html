Know the Signs

Does Your Sewer Line Need Full Replacement?

Full replacement means a new line, not another patch or repair on the same section. Here's how to know you're actually at that point:

Repairs Keep Failing on the Same Section

A section that's already been patched or relined and is still failing has run out of good pipe left to repair. Another patch just buys a little time.

Cracking or Root Intrusion Runs the Length of the Line

When camera footage shows damage spread across most of the pipe, not just one weak spot, lining or patching a single section won't fix what's failing everywhere else.

The Pipe Has Collapsed or a Section Is Offset

A collapsed or badly offset pipe can't be patched back into shape. Water isn't moving through it the way it should, and a repair won't change that.

How We Decide

Repair or Replacement? How to Tell the Difference

The line between a repair and a full replacement usually comes down to how much of the pipe is affected, not just what's wrong with it. Here's how the two usually compare:

Repair Usually HoldsFull Replacement Is Likely
One confirmed crack, joint separation, or root intrusion on camera, isolated to a single pointCracking or root intrusion running across most of the pipe's length
First time this section has needed attentionThe same section has already been repaired and is still failing
Slow drains, gurgling, or backups that clear once we rod or fix the specific spotBackups that keep coming back no matter how many times the line is cleared
The rest of the pipe is structurally sound around the damaged areaThe pipe has collapsed, or a section is offset

A camera inspection is what turns this from a guess into an answer, and we run one before recommending either.
